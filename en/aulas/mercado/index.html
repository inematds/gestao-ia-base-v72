Lesson 5 · 20 min

How the market is reorganizing

McKinsey, Deloitte, Microsoft and Gartner publish about the agentic company almost every month, and those numbers reach your desk as a decision: cut, hire, promise. In this lesson you read what each one says and learn to separate research, self-reported experience and forecast. At the end, the owner of Horizonte shows up with a report, and you are the one who answers.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1What McKinsey, Deloitte, Microsoft and Gartner say about managing with agents
  2. 2The four questions of critical reading: who publishes it, how they measured, is it a forecast, what applies to me
  3. 3Why taking management off the org chart does not take the work away
  4. 4Answering a "McKinsey said that..." without agreeing or fighting

Goal: read a consulting or vendor report and separate what is research, what is self-reported experience and what is forecast.

02 · Four frames, four kinds of claim

Each consultancy and vendor sells its own frame. See what each one says and what kind of claim it is.

1. McKinsey

McKinsey describes the "agentic organization" (2025–2026): small teams built around an outcome as the unit of the organization, with 2 to 5 people supervising 50 to 100 specialized agents, and accountability designed in from the start (accountability by design). It also says the span of control may narrow at first and widen later, with supervision tools.

Type: self-reported experience from the consultancy, not a measured study. It is what the firm observes and proposes to its clients.

SheetWho publishes it: a consultancy that sells reorganization. How they measured: reporting. Forecast: no. What I take away: the unit of management becomes the team built around an outcome.

2. Deloitte

In Tech Trends 2026, Deloitte treats agents as a "silicon-based" workforce: integration with data and operations, graduated autonomy with human supervision triggers, and training for supervisors. In Human Capital Trends 2025, it discusses the future of the middle manager.

Type: consultancy view, with trends and recommendations. It works as vocabulary and as a checklist, not as a number.

SheetWho publishes it: a consultancy. How they measured: trends and cases. Forecast: in part. What I take away: whoever supervises an agent also needs to be trained.

3. Microsoft

Work Trend Index 2026 (05/2026): 20 thousand people in 10 countries. 16% are "Frontier Professionals", who use agents in multi-step workflows; 58% do work they could not do a year ago (80% among the Frontier); when the manager uses AI in front of the team, perceived value goes up 17 points.

Type: survey with a sample, run by the company that sells the tool. An analysis by The Microsoft Cloud Blog (05/2026) reminds readers that these numbers should not carry a business case on their own.

SheetWho publishes it: an AI vendor. How they measured: opinion survey with a large sample. Forecast: no. What I take away: the manager setting the example matters; the 58% is not from my company.

4. Gartner

Forecast made in October 2024: by the end of 2026, 1 in 5 organizations will use AI to flatten their structure and cut more than half of middle management roles. In 2026, in the Hype Cycle for agentic AI, the same Gartner recorded that only 17% of companies have agents in use.

Type: forecast, with a deadline that is coming due now. Critics point out that taking a layer off the org chart does not eliminate the work it did: coordinating, reviewing, training the people who join. That is where the agent manager comes in.

SheetWho publishes it: a research consultancy. How it measured: analyst forecast. Forecast: yes, for the end of 2026. What I take from it: list the management work before cutting the layer.

03 · Template to copy: critical reading sheet

One sheet for each report or news item that reaches your desk looking like a decision. It takes ten minutes and saves a whole meeting of opinion against opinion.

Critical reading sheet
CRITICAL READING SHEET · <report title> · <who publishes it> · <date>
Arrived through: <who brought it and which decision it is meant to justify>

1. WHO PUBLISHES IT AND WHAT THEY SELL
   <consultancy / vendor / institute / press> · sells: <...>
   What they would have no interest in showing: <...>

2. HOW IT MEASURED
   [ ] survey with a sample: <how many, where, when, which question>
   [ ] self-reported experience (what they saw with clients)
   [ ] case study   [ ] opinion / column   [ ] doesn't say

3. IS IT A FORECAST?
   [ ] no   [ ] yes: made on <date>, for <deadline>. Can we check it yet? <yes/no>

4. THE NUMBER THAT WILL BE USED
   "<number and exact sentence>" · type: <survey / account / forecast / opinion>
   Definition used by the report: <what counts as "uses", "integrates", "adopts">

5. WHAT APPLIES TO ME
   What I take from it (1 sentence): <...>
   What I need to measure here before deciding: <measure, for how long, who>
Prompt · separate fact from forecast
You are a skeptical analyst. I will paste an excerpt from a market report.
Do not summarize and do not give an opinion on the subject: classify the claims.

Excerpt:
"""<paste here>"""

For each claim with a number or trend:
1. Say whether it is a survey with a sample, self-reported experience, case study,
   forecast or opinion. If the excerpt doesn't make it clear, say "not stated".
2. If it is a forecast, point out when it was made and for when.
3. Point out the definition used (what counts as "adopt", "use", "integrate").
4. List what I would need to measure in my company to get the
   equivalent number.
Do not invent data that is not in the excerpt.
Simulation: illustrative example

Work Trend Index 2026 · Microsoft · 05/2026 · brought by Carlos to expand Hora to collections (Horizonte, fictional study company)

  • Who publishes it: an AI vendor, which sells the tool whose usage it measures.
  • How it measured: survey of 20 thousand people in 10 countries.
  • Forecast: no.
  • Number to be used: "58% do work they couldn't do a year ago" · opinion survey, self-reported by the respondents themselves.
  • What I take from it: a manager who uses AI in front of the team changes the perception of value.
  • What to measure here: two weeks of requests accepted without correction and time from request to invoicing, before expanding Hora; owner, Patrícia.
  1. All five parts are filled in; "doesn't say" is also an answer.
  2. The number is in quotes, exactly as in the report, with the type next to it.
  3. The last line has a measure, a deadline and a name.
  4. Common mistake: reading only the headline. The headline almost never says how the number was measured.
