Lesson 1 · 16 min

The third object of management

For forty years, management coordinated two things: people and processes. Now a third has arrived, and it works alongside your team. In this lesson you walk through the five eras of management, see what each one added, and find out why 2027 is not about creating agents, but about answering for them.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1What each era of management added and what still holds
  2. 2The three changes that make the agent a management object
  3. 3Why speeding up the old process only delivers the error faster
  4. 4Find, in your company, the management questions that still have no owner

Goal: explain why management in 2027 coordinates people, processes and agents, with an example of your own in each of the five eras.

01 · Five eras of management

1/6

People

People management organized work into roles: each with responsibility, tools, limits and evaluation. Who gives a discount, up to how much, who double-checks. That logic hasn't gone away: almost everything you will do with agents comes from it.

Scroll the page: each step below lights up its part of the diagram.

  1. 1/6

    People

    People management organized work into roles: each with responsibility, tools, limits and evaluation. Who gives a discount, up to how much, who double-checks. That logic hasn't gone away: almost everything you will do with agents comes from it.

  2. 2/6

    Processes

    Then companies learned to design, document, train for and run processes. The process became an asset: you can teach it to newcomers and hold the people who run it accountable. The risk of this era is treating the process as something that can't be touched.

  3. 3/6

    Digital

    Digital transformation automated what was already designed: design, automate, monitor. Systems carry out fixed steps; when something falls outside the rule, they stop and wait for a person.

  4. 4/6

    Build

    In 2025 and 2026 the question was how to create agents: prompts, skills, context, tools, memory. Many companies learned to build an agent in an afternoon. Few learned what to do with it the next day.

  5. 5/6

    Manage

    In 2027 the company coordinates humans, agents, models, processes and compute budget all at once. The question changes from "how do I build it?" to "who answers for it, what does it do on its own, how do I know it's good and how much does each result cost?".

  6. 6/6

    The whole ladder

    People, processes, digital, building and management. The last era uses all the earlier ones: the role comes from people management, the design comes from process management, the measure comes from digital. What it adds is a new worker, who needs a boss.

The dates of the eras are approximate and come from the INEMA AI Management Area; they are meant to give you a sense of place, not to set a calendar.

02 · Three changes and one question

Horizonte Distribuidora (fictional study company) went through all five eras. See where it got stuck in the fifth.

1. Building got easy

Any team can build an agent in an afternoon. What holds the company back is what comes after: who answers for it, what it can do on its own, how you measure whether it's good and how much each result costs.

These four questions aren't technical. They're the same ones a manager asks when a new employee arrives, and nobody expects IT to answer for them.

At HorizonteRafael, from the outsourced IT, switched Hora on on July 1st. Three months later nobody can say who approved the 35% discount she gave a customer.

2. Don't speed up the old process

Putting an agent into a flow designed for people fifteen years ago only delivers the error faster. The question that changes everything: if I had humans, agents and automations today, would this process still exist this way?

Taking the process apart before automating is the subject of the G2 course. Here it's enough to know the question exists.

At HorizonteHora went down the same path as before: it notes the order, sends it to the warehouse and someone checks it later, if there's time. Only now it's hundreds of conversations a week.

3. The process stops being frozen

With agents measuring their own execution, the process becomes a living system: it runs, observes, proposes an improvement, tests, validates and promotes. INEMA calls this cycle LOOP-R, and it is the subject of the G5 course.

For this to work, someone needs to have measured how things were before. Without a starting point, there's no way to say it improved.

At HorizonteHora's dashboard shows the volume rising every week, but the collection began on the day she was switched on. There's no way to compare with the time before.

4. The third object

People and processes stay on the manager's desk. The agent is the third object, and it brings others along: the model that drives it, the context it reads, the permissions it receives and the cost of each run.

An IT Forum column (2026, opinion) summed up the moment this way: this is the year companies stop building agents and start operating them. It's opinion, not measurement, but it describes the shift in the question well.

Three questions with no owner at HorizonteWho is accountable for Hora's discount? Who decides, in November, whether she starts charging customers? Who knew about the four AI tools the team uses that IT doesn't know about?

Simulation: illustrative example

Sales · Horizonte Distribuidora · fictional study company

  • People: salespeople with a price list and a discount limit agreed with Carlos.
  • Processes: the GestorPlus ERP (inventory, invoices, accounts) and the step-by-step for an order.
  • Digital: the Horizonte Pedidos app, WhatsApp Business and the cloud spreadsheets.
  • Building agents: Hora (since 07/01/2026), the sales team's email assistant and the automatic customer-service summary at 6 p.m. Plus four AI tools used on people's own initiative.
  • Managing: who is accountable for Hora's discount? who decides whether she moves into collections? how do we know if orders got better, if nobody measured before July?
  1. Each era has an example from your area, not from the market.
  2. Era 4 includes what people use without IT knowing. If the list came only from IT, ask the team.
  3. Each era 5 question can be answered with a name, a number or a rule.
  4. Common mistake: calling every automation an "agent." An automation follows fixed steps; an agent chooses the path within limits.

04 · Practice

First at Horizonte, to practice. Then in your area, for real.

1 · The Horizonte timeline

20 min

Open the lab's IT inventory and also read the 22 answers from the internal survey. Copy the template and build the timeline for Horizonte's sales team without looking at the example next to it. In era 5, write three questions that nobody answers today. Then compare with the example.

Done when: the timeline has all five eras, each one with a system or fact taken from the lab, era 4 mentions at least one tool that IT doesn't know about, and era 5 has three questions that can be answered with a name, a number or a rule.

2 · The timeline for your area

25 min

Do the same with your area. For era 4, ask two people on the team which AI tools they use at work, including on their phones. Run the review prompt and fix what it points out. Finish with the next step: who answers the first question, by when.

Done when: your timeline has an example of your own in each of the five eras, era 4 includes what you heard from at least two people, and the first question with no owner has a name and a date for being answered.

Lab pieces: IT inventory and internal survey and Hora dashboard (fictional study company).
