Lesson 4 · 18 min

The 8 skills of the AI manager

Strategy, work architecture, delegation, orchestration, governance, economics, evaluation and evolution. The first four decide what the agent will do; the last four, whether it keeps doing it. In this lesson you give yourself a score from 0 to 3 on each one, with one piece of evidence per score, and choose which course in the program to take next.

Start the lesson

  1. 1The 8 skills, in the order a manager uses them
  2. 2The question each skill answers

  4. 4Choose the next course by risk, not by curiosity

Goal: score yourself from 0 to 3 on the 8 skills of the AI manager, with one piece of evidence for each score, and choose the next course in the program.

Scroll the page: each step below lights up its part of the diagram.

  1. 1/9

    Strategy

    Question: what result do we want? Result first, tool second. Don't start with the model or the platform. This is the skill of this course (G0): knowing what you want before you turn anything on.

  2. 2/9

    Architecture

    Question: should this process exist this way at all? Architecture of the work: eliminate, simplify, redesign, and only then automate. Step by step, decide what is a person, an agent, a simple automation, or nothing. This is course G2.

  3. 3/9

    Delegation

    Question: what exactly am I handing to the agent? Goal, responsibility, context, tools, deadline, and evidence of completion. Delegating means handing the agent work it can finish and you can check. This is course G3.

  4. 4/9

    Orchestration

    Question: one agent or several? One agent or several, in series or in parallel, with a person in the middle, a supervisor agent, or a reviewer. Split only when there is a signal for it. Also in course G3.

  5. 5/9

    Governance

    Question: does it act on its own, confirm first, or never do it? Permissions, identity, credentials, logging, and traceability. Authority limits before autonomy: what the agent does on its own, what it confirms, and what it never executes. This is course G5.

  6. 6/9

    Economics

    Question: how much does the result cost? Model, tokens, time, infrastructure: cost per run, per task, and per result. A cheap agent that gets it wrong and needs two tries cost double the price list. This is course G4.

  7. 7/9

    Evaluation

    Question: is it good, compared to what? Quality, time, cost, failures, rework, autonomy, human intervention, and business result, always compared to a starting point. An agent gets a performance review. This is course G5.

  8. 8/9

    Evolution

    Question: what did we learn? The company stops asking only "did the task finish?" and starts asking what it learned from it. A tested improvement is promoted; one that made things worse is rolled back. This is LOOP-R, in course G5.

  9. 9/9

    All eight together

    Strategy, architecture, delegation, and orchestration decide what the agent will do. Governance, economics, evaluation, and evolution decide whether it keeps doing it. People, which doesn't show up as a competency, has its own course: G1.

The 8 competencies belong to INEMA's AI Management area. The 0-to-3 scale is a tool for this course, for conversation and for choosing a track, not an exam.

02 · How to give yourself an honest score

Four steps. Patrícia, from Horizonte (a fictional study company), is the example before you rate yourself.

1. The 0-to-3 scale

0: I've never done it and don't know where to start. 1: I understand the question, but haven't applied it yet. 2: I've done it at least once, and I have the file or record. 3: I do it as a routine and can teach someone else.

The scale measures what you do, not what you've read. Having watched a video about governance is a 1; having a reviewed list of permissions is a 2.

At HorizontePatrícia knows Hora should have a discount cap (a 1 in governance), but the cap doesn't exist in the tool yet.

2. One piece of evidence per score

For every 2 or 3, write down the evidence: a file, a record, a dated decision. If there is no evidence, the score is a 1. It sounds strict, and it is: self-assessment without evidence always comes out higher.

For scores of 0 and 1, write down what is missing. Your track comes from that sentence.

At HorizonteEvaluation: Hora's dashboard exists, but it has no baseline and satisfaction comes from about 6% of customers. A 1, not a 2.

3. The two halves

A low score in the first four means the agent does the wrong thing or does it the wrong way. A low score in the last four means nobody knows whether it is doing well, what it costs, or how to improve it.

A manager who only invests in the first half builds agents that nobody can maintain. One who only invests in the second precisely measures work that was badly designed.

At HorizonteIn the first half, Patrícia scores 1 in strategy and delegation and 0 in architecture and orchestration. In the second, 1 in governance and evaluation and 0 in economics and evolution. Both halves are weak: 2 of 12 in each.

4. Choose by risk

The program's suggested order is G0, G2, G3, G4, G1, G5. But if a low-scoring competency is causing harm right now in your process, start with its course. Present risk beats suggested order.

Write the reason for your choice in one sentence. Three months from now, redo the self-assessment and compare.

At HorizontePatrícia chooses G3: Hora gives discounts with no cap and creates large orders with no approval today. G2 comes right after, to redesign the order.

04 · Practice

First assess Patrícia, to calibrate the ruler. Then assess yourself.

1 · Assess Patrícia

15 min

Using only what is in the lab (Hora's prompt, the configuration, the conversations and the dashboard), give Patrícia a score from 0 to 3 on each competency, with the evidence or what is missing. Only then open the example next to it and note where you differed.

Done when: all eight scores are given, each with one sentence of evidence or of what is missing taken from a lab piece, and you noted at least one difference from the example and the reason.

2 · Your self-assessment

20 min

Fill in the template for yourself. Run the skeptical prompt and lower the scores it shows have no evidence. Choose the next course in the program based on the competency at highest risk in your process, and put the date to redo this in three months on your calendar.

Done when: you have eight scores, eight pieces of evidence or "what is missing", the two sums, one chosen course (G1 to G5) with the reason in one sentence, and the reassessment date on your calendar.

Lab pieces: WhatsApp agent and Hora dashboard (fictional study company).
